# Route 1: Foundational Guidance
### Understanding Campus Entry Points & Academic Structures

---

Before reaching out to labs or applying on portals, it helps to understand how undergraduate research is structured at UC Berkeley. Research entry generally falls into three main mechanisms: **Formal Campus Programs**, **Independent Academic Credit**, and **Volunteer/Shadowing Positions**.

---

## 1. Structured Campus Programs

Structured programs offer defined pipelines, application timelines, and often mentorship frameworks.

### [Undergraduate Research Apprentice Program (URAP)](https://research.berkeley.edu/urap/)
* **What It Is:** Berkeley’s central platform for matching undergrads with faculty research projects.
* **Timeline:** Applications open during the first two weeks of the Fall and Spring semesters.
* **How It Works:** Principal Investigators (PIs) are faculty who are responsible for the management of a research project. They list specific positions with required skills and time commitments. You can submit applications for up to 3 projects per term.
* **Credit/Pay:** Accepted students typically enroll in **UGBA/LS 198** or receive transcript notation for their work.

### Key Campuswide Programs & Portals

 **[Office of Undergraduate Research and Scholarships (OURS)](https://research.berkeley.edu/)** — The central hub for undergraduate research at UC Berkeley. OURS offers workshops, peer advising, and oversight for major campus funding programs.
 
 **[Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship (SURF)](https://research.berkeley.edu/surf/)** — Provides funding for UC Berkeley undergraduates (primarily in the College of Letters & Science) to spend the summer conducting concentrated research in preparation for a senior thesis.
 
 **[Haas Scholars Program](https://research.berkeley.edu/haas-scholars/benefits/)** — A prestigious cohort-based program offering up to $14,800 in funding, faculty mentorship, and a seminar series for high-achieving seniors with financial need undertaking an independent senior capstone or thesis project.

## 2. Independent Study Credit (199 / 99 Courses)

If you are working with a faculty member outside of a formal program like URAP, you can receive academic units for your research hours.

* **Course Numbering:**
  * **99 / 98:** Lower-division independent study credit (freshmen/sophomores).
  * **199 / 198:** Upper-division independent study credit (juniors/seniors).
* **Unit Formula:** Standard university policy equates **1 unit of credit to approximately 3–4 hours of weekly lab or research work** over a 15-week semester (e.g., 3 units = 9–12 hours/week).
* **Enrollment Process:**
  1. Secure approval from your Principal Investigator (PI) or direct lab supervisor.
  2. Complete your home department’s Independent Study Form (often available on the department’s academic advising page).
  3. Submit the signed form to obtain a class enrollment code before the campus add/drop deadline (usually Week 4 of the semester).

---

## 3. Volunteer & Shadowing Positions

* **How It Works:** You join a lab without academic credit or compensation, focusing on learning protocols, attending lab meetings, and shadowing senior graduate students or postdocs.
* **Time Commitment:** Typically lower and more flexible (5–10 hours/week).
* **Strategic Value:** Volunteering is one of the most effective "low-friction" ways to demonstrate reliability. Many lab volunteers naturally transition into paid positions, funded summer projects, or upper-division 199 credit in subsequent semesters.
